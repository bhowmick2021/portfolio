Legacy BI to GCP/BigQuery transformation

On-premises BI moved to BigQuery with no production disruption.

Period2020–22
RoleSr. Lead – BI Tech (led)
StackGCP, BigQuery, Tableau, SQL, Airflow/Composer, Pub/Sub
AttributionEmployer work, not external client work

Problem

On-premises BI infrastructure limited cost, availability and self-serve analytics.

My contribution

  • Led architecture, rollout planning, rollback and business-continuity checks.
  • Standardized ETL, data modelling and access governance as part of the move.

Decisions

  • BigQuery as the target platform.
  • A governed cutover: rollout plan, rollback strategy, change management and continuity checks.
  • Standardize ETL, modelling and access rules during the move so old problems were not carried across.

Delivered change

  • Zero-disruption cutover to GCP/BigQuery.
  • Standardized ETL, data models and access governance.
  • The base for self-serve analytics that the current platform builds on.

Validation

Business-continuity checks and rollback checkpoints before cutover; the outcome is no production disruption.

Approved outcome

Lower cost, higher availability and a base for self-serve analytics. Stated qualitatively: no percentage is claimed for this migration.

Not claimed

No cost or availability percentages. Historical work, not AI-assisted.

In my words, by audience

For Recruiters

I led the move of our BI platform from on-premises infrastructure to GCP and BigQuery. I owned the architecture and rollout plan and designed the rollback and continuity checks so reporting stayed available. We cut over with no production disruption and left the business with lower cost, higher availability and a base for self-serve analytics. It set the platform direction I still own.

The Builder

I led our on-prem BI to BigQuery migration. My part was architecture and the cutover plan: what moves when, how we roll back, and what we check before declaring success. I standardized ETL and modelling during the move and we finished with zero production disruption. I describe the result as lower cost and higher availability rather than quote a number I cannot defend.

The Consultant

At Radix, in my employer role, I led a move from an on-premises BI platform to BigQuery without disrupting the business. For a team considering a similar move I can start with a short written assessment of what to migrate, how to protect continuity and what to fix first. Any implementation would be a separate, agreed scope.
